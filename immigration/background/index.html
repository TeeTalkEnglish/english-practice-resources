01

The situation

You are preparing for life in Australia with your husband and his family.

In an English interview, you may need to explain your background, your relationship, your family situation, and your plans for Australia. You do not need perfect answers. You need clear, honest, natural answers.

Use your own real information. If you do not know an exact date, say so. Keep your answers clear and direct.

This lesson is for English-speaking practice only. These are practice questions, not an official list of Australian immigration interview questions. Always answer truthfully and use your own real information.

About you

About You

1. What is your full name?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to confirm your identity and basic personal information.

Simple answer pattern

My name is [full name]. I am from [place].

Useful vocabulary
  • full name: your complete name
  • currently: at the moment
  • country of birth: the place where you were born

2. Where were you born?

What the interviewer is asking: They want basic details about your background.

Simple answer pattern

I was born in [city/country].

Useful vocabulary
  • born: came into the world
  • hometown: the town or city where you are from
  • country: a nation or state

3. Where do you currently live?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know your living situation and where you live now.

Simple answer pattern

I currently live in [city/country]. I live with [family/partner/friends].

Useful vocabulary
  • currently: at the moment
  • live: to have your home in a place
  • address: your home location

4. What do you currently do?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know your everyday work situation.

Simple answer pattern

I currently work as a [job]. I have experience in [field].

Useful vocabulary
  • occupation: your job or work
  • work experience: experience in a job
  • currently: now

5. What did you study?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know about your education background.

Simple answer pattern

I studied [subject or qualification]. I completed [course or certificate].

Useful vocabulary
  • study: to learn at school or university
  • qualification: a certificate or degree
  • course: a class or subject

6. What kind of work experience do you have?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to understand your practical work background.

Simple answer pattern

I have experience in [job or industry]. I have worked in [type of work] for [time].

Useful vocabulary
  • experience: practice or time working
  • industry: a type of work area
  • field: a work or study area

7. How long have you worked in your current or previous job?

What the interviewer is asking: They want a simple timeline of your work experience.

Simple answer pattern

I have worked in this type of work for [number] years. I started in [year or time period].

Useful vocabulary
  • previous job: a job you had before
  • current job: the job you have now
  • for [time]: during a period

8. Why do you want to move to Australia?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to understand your motivation and future plans.

Simple answer pattern

I want to move to Australia because I want to live with my husband and build a future together. I also want to make a stable life for our family.

Useful vocabulary
  • move: relocate to another place
  • future: the time ahead
  • stable: secure and steady

Work plans

Questions About Your Work Plans

1. What kind of work do you plan to do in Australia?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to understand your employment plans.

Simple answer pattern

I plan to work in a role related to [food production / manufacturing / meat processing / general work]. I am willing to learn and work hard.

Useful vocabulary
  • employment: paid work
  • job: work you do
  • role: a type of work position

2. Have you worked as a butcher before?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know whether you have relevant experience in meat work.

Simple answer pattern

Yes, I have worked as a [butcher / meat worker / in food production] before. I have experience in [cutting / trimming / packaging].

Useful vocabulary
  • butcher: a person who prepares and cuts meat
  • meat processing: preparing meat in a factory or plant
  • cutting: dividing meat using a knife

3. What experience do you have in meat processing?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know if you have practical knowledge working with meat.

Simple answer pattern

I have experience in meat processing, including [packing / trimming / handling meat / working on a production line].

Useful vocabulary
  • processing plant: a place where food is prepared
  • production line: a sequence of work steps
  • trimming: cutting away extra parts

4. What duties have you performed in your previous work?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to understand the tasks you know how to do.

Simple answer pattern

In my previous work, I performed tasks such as [cutting / packaging / cleaning / checking hygiene / working with equipment].

Useful vocabulary
  • duty: a task you do at work
  • packaging: putting products in containers
  • equipment: tools or machines used at work

5. What equipment have you used?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know whether you understand work tools and workplace tasks.

Simple answer pattern

I have used [knives / cutting tools / packaging equipment / cleaning equipment].

Useful vocabulary
  • equipment: tools or machines
  • knife skills: ability to use knives safely and correctly
  • protective equipment: clothing or gear that keeps workers safe

6. What do you know about workplace safety?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to see that you understand safe work habits and basic rules.

Simple answer pattern

I understand that workplace safety is very important. I follow instructions, use protective equipment, and keep the work area clean and safe.

Useful vocabulary
  • workplace safety: safe practices at work
  • hygiene: cleanliness and health rules
  • supervisor: the person who gives instructions at work

7. Why are you interested in this type of work?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know your motivation for this work.

Simple answer pattern

I am interested in this type of work because I have experience in it and I am willing to learn more. I like practical work.

Useful vocabulary
  • interested in: wanting to do or learn about something
  • practical: hands-on and useful
  • experience: practice gained from work

8. Where do you expect to work?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know where you hope to find work.

Simple answer pattern

I expect to work in a [meat-processing plant / food production facility / processing environment] if suitable work is available.

Useful vocabulary
  • facility: a place for work or production
  • meat-processing plant: a place where meat is prepared
  • environment: the surrounding work setting

9. What do you know about working in a meat-processing environment?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know if you understand the general demands of this type of workplace.

Simple answer pattern

I know that working in a meat-processing environment requires focus, good hygiene, teamwork, and attention to safety instructions.

Useful vocabulary
  • teamwork: working together with others
  • hygiene: cleanliness and safe practices
  • shift: a period of work time

10. What will you do if you do not find work immediately?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to see whether you have a realistic and practical plan.

Simple answer pattern

If I do not find work immediately, I will continue looking for suitable work, improve my skills, and stay positive while I look for the right opportunity.

Useful vocabulary
  • opportunity: a possible chance
  • suitable: appropriate
  • skills: abilities you have learned

11. What are your long-term career plans in Australia?

What the interviewer is asking: They want to know your future goals and whether you have a realistic plan.

Simple answer pattern

My long-term plan is to work steadily, build experience, support my family, and work towards a stable future in Australia.

Useful vocabulary
  • long-term: over a long period of time

  • stable: secure and dependable

Answer strategy

How to Answer

  1. Answer directly. Start with the main answer.
  2. Give one or two useful details. Do not turn every question into a long speech.
  3. Be honest when you do not know. It is okay to say, “I’m not sure of the exact date,” or “As far as I know...”

Question: What do you plan to do in Australia?

Example pattern: I plan to work as a [butcher/meat-processing worker] if I am able to obtain suitable employment. I also want to build a life with my husband and become part of our family life in Australia.

This is only an example. Replace it with your own real circumstances and honest details.

Common mistakes

Avoid These Mistakes

Memorizing a script

Use your own words. The aim is to speak naturally and clearly, not to recite a perfect answer.

Giving answers that are too long

Answer the question and stop when you have given enough information. One or two useful details are usually enough.

Guessing dates or facts

Never invent information. If you do not know an exact detail, say that clearly and answer honestly.

Saying things that do not match your documents

Your answers should be truthful and consistent with your real circumstances and application documents.

Calling your husband's children something inaccurate

Use the relationship term that is actually true for your circumstances. For example, say “your husband's children” if that is accurate.

Panicking when asked an unexpected question

Pause, ask for clarification if needed, and answer honestly. A calm answer is better than a rushed one.
