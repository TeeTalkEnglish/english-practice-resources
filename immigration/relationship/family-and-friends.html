Family & Friends

Practise describing parents, siblings, relatives, close friends, family visits and celebrations.

01

The situation

Talk about the people around you.

Explain who you have met, how often you see family and friends, what you do together and how your families relate to the marriage.

Family relationships are personal. Be accurate and respectful.

Essential vocabulary

People and shared occasions.

01

parents / siblings

Your mother, father, brothers and sisters.

02

in-laws

Your mother-in-law and father-in-law.

03

relatives

Members of your wider family.

04

close friends

Friends you know and see often.

05

family gathering

A shared meal or celebration.

06

get along with

Have a friendly relationship with someone.

Questions you may encounter

Give real family details.

  • Have you met your partner's family?
  • How often do you see them?
  • Who are your partner's closest friends?
  • Have your families met?
  • What does your family think about your marriage?
  • What do you do at family gatherings?
  • Who does your partner get along with?

Grammar focus

Present perfect and frequency.

Have you met...?

I've met his parents twice. We usually visit them on holidays. His closest friend lives nearby.

Role-play

Answer with a relationship detail.

Question

Have you met your partner's family?

Answer shape

Yes, I met [people] in [place/time]. We usually [activity] when we visit them.

Speaking challenge

Introduce three people.

Describe your partner's closest family member, a friend and how you know them.

Example only

I've met my husband's parents and two sisters. We see them several times a year, especially on birthdays. His closest friend is Daniel, whom he met at university. They still play tennis together.

Keep it genuine

Relationships are individual.

This lesson is for English practice only, not legal or immigration advice.
