Your Financial Relationship

Learn to explain joint or separate accounts, household expenses, bills, savings and financial support honestly.

The situation

Explain how money works for you.

Describe joint bank accounts, separate accounts, rent, mortgage, bills, savings, insurance, transfers and contributions.

Separate finances are a valid arrangement. Never create or claim financial evidence that is not real.

Essential vocabulary

Language for shared expenses.

01

joint bank account

An account held by both partners.

02

separate account

An account managed by one person.

03

household expenses

Costs of running a home.

04

contribution

Money each person puts toward a cost.

05

financial support

Money provided to help a partner.

06

savings / insurance

Money kept for the future or protection from risk.

Questions you may encounter

Explain your real arrangement.

  • Do you have a joint bank account?
  • How do you share household expenses?
  • Who usually pays the bills?
  • Do you financially support each other?
  • Do you have shared savings or insurance?
  • Do you transfer money to each other?
  • Why do you keep your finances separate?

Grammar focus

Explain reasons with because.

Shared or separate

We use a joint account for rent because it is convenient. We keep our other accounts separate because we had them before we married.

Role-play

Give a direct, complete answer.

Question

Do you share your finances?

Answer shape

[Yes/No]. We [arrangement] because [real reason]. We share [expense], and [person] pays [bill].

Speaking challenge

Explain your finances in one minute.

Say whether accounts are joint or separate, then explain two real shared expenses.

Example only

We do not have a joint bank account because we keep our personal finances separate. However, we share our household expenses. I pay the electricity and internet bills, and my partner pays the rent. We both contribute to our groceries.

Keep it genuine

Honesty is the useful language.

This lesson is for English practice only, not legal or immigration advice.
