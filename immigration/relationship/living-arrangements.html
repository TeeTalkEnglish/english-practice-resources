Relationship & marriage Lesson 05

Living Arrangements

Explain where you live, who shares the home, household responsibilities, or how you manage a long-distance arrangement.

01

The situation

Explain the arrangement you really have.

Describe your type of home, bedrooms, household items, rent or lease, utilities and responsibilities. If you live apart, explain why and how you stay connected.

There is no single correct arrangement. This lesson is for English practice only, not legal advice.

Home vocabulary

Words for where you live.

01

live together

We share the same home.

02

rent / lease

We rent our apartment and have a one-year lease.

03

utilities

Electricity, water and internet bills.

04

household items

Furniture and things used at home.

05

responsibilities

We share household responsibilities.

06

long-distance

We currently live in different places.

Questions you may encounter

Describe your home clearly.

  • Where do you live?
  • Do you live together?
  • Who else lives in the house?
  • How long have you lived there?
  • How many bedrooms are there?
  • Who pays the rent and utilities?
  • How do you divide household responsibilities?
  • What is your arrangement while you live apart?

Grammar focus

There is, there are and have lived.

Describe the home

There are two bedrooms in our apartment. We have lived there since March. We share the rent and utilities.

Role-play

Answer with place, people and time.

Question

Where do you live?

Answer shape

We live in [type of home] in [place]. [People] live there. We have lived there for [time].

Speaking challenge

Describe your current home.

Include the location, type of home, people, length of time and one responsibility.

Example only

We live in a two-bedroom apartment in Melbourne. Just the two of us live there, and we have lived there for eighteen months. We share the rent and internet bill, and I usually do the cooking while my partner does the laundry.

Keep it genuine

Every arrangement is different.

This lesson is for English practice only, not legal or immigration advice.
