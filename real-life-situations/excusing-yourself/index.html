Excusing Yourself: Leaving Politely

Learn how to step away or leave a conversation politely, whether you need a quick break, have somewhere to be, or prefer to keep your reason private.

Learning goal

Leave clearly, kindly, and naturally.

Learn different ways to excuse yourself when you need to step away or leave. Choose a phrase based on urgency, politeness, and how much detail you want to give.

Useful phrases

Keep the reason short and natural.

Step away briefly

  • Excuse me, I'll be right back.
  • Can you give me a minute?
  • I just need to step away for a moment.

Use these for the bathroom, getting something, taking a call, or speaking privately.

Leave

  • I need to get going.
  • I have to head out.
  • I should probably get going.
  • I have to run.casual

Give a reason

  • Something came up.
  • I need to take care of something.
  • I have an appointment.
  • Something urgent came up at home.

Keep the reason private

  • I have something I need to take care of.
  • I have a personal matter to deal with.
  • I'm sorry, but I can't stay.

Language notes

Small words change the feeling.

Useful starters

Excuse me = a polite way to interrupt or get attention.

Sorry = common when interrupting or leaving.

I need to... = expresses a need.

I have to... = a stronger sense of necessity.

Useful meanings

I should... = softer.

Something came up = an unexpected situation happened.

I have to run = a casual way to say “I need to leave.”

Politeness levels

Match your words to the relationship.

Casual

“I have to run.”

“Give me a second.”

Neutral

“I need to get going.”

“Excuse me, I'll be right back.”

Polite

“Could you give me a minute?”

“I'm afraid I have to leave early.”

Real-life dialogues

See the phrases in context.

Bathroom / breakA: “Excuse me, I'll be right back. I just need to step away for a moment.”
B: “Sure, no problem.”
Important callA: “Sorry, I need to take this call. Do you mind if I step outside?”
B: “Of course. We'll wait for you.”
Going home earlyA: “I'm afraid I have to leave early. I'm not feeling very well.”
B: “I'm sorry to hear that. Take care.”
Urgent at homeA: “Something urgent came up at home, so I need to leave.”
B: “I hope everything is all right.”
Ending a chatA: “It was great talking with you, but I need to get going.”
B: “It was great talking with you too. See you later!”
