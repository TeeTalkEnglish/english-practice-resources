Getting Out of Uncomfortable Situations Politely

Learn how to end conversations, set boundaries, and leave uncomfortable situations naturally.

Why this matters

Being polite does not mean staying in an uncomfortable situation.

Important: In a new workplace and a new country, you may want to be friendly and polite. But being friendly does not mean being available. Being polite does not mean saying yes. You do not have to answer every personal question. You do not have to continue every conversation. You do not have to stay somewhere that makes you uncomfortable.

Useful phrases

Choose the phrase that matches the moment.

A. Getting back to work

  • “Sorry, I need to get back to work.”return
  • “I’m a bit busy right now.”brief
  • “I need to finish this first.”clear
  • “I need to get back to my station.”workplace
  • “Can we talk later?”soft
  • “I’ll catch up with you later.”friendly

B. Polite exit

  • “Excuse me, I need to step away for a moment.”brief
  • “Sorry, I need to get going.”exit
  • “I’m going to head out.”casual
  • “It was nice talking to you.”warm
  • “I’m going to catch up with my friends.”distance

C. Creating distance

  • “I need to make a phone call.”reason
  • “I have something I need to take care of.”private
  • “I’m going to check on something.”busy
  • “Let me get back to my friends.”exit

D. Personal questions

  • “I’d rather not talk about that.”soft
  • “I’d prefer to keep that private.”firm
  • “I’m not comfortable talking about that.”clear
  • “Anyway, I should get going.”leave

E. When someone won’t stop

  • “Sorry, I really have to go.”firm
  • “I need to get back to what I was doing.”direct
  • “I’m going to leave now. Take care.”final

F. Unwanted approach

  • “Sorry, I’m not interested.”clear
  • “I’d rather be left alone.”boundary
  • “Excuse me, I need some space.”distance

G. Pushy people / salespeople

  • “No, thank you. I’m just looking.”polite
  • “No, thanks.”short
  • “I’m not interested, thank you.”firm

Polite → Firm → Leave

You do not have to jump straight to the strongest response.

Example 1

Polite: “Sorry, I need to get back to work.”

Then: “I need to finish this first.”
Example 2

Firm: “I’d prefer to keep that private.”

Then: “I’m not comfortable discussing that.”
Example 3

Final: “I need to leave now. Take care.”

Use this when the person is still not listening.
Start with a polite phrase. If the person keeps going, become clearer. If the situation is uncomfortable or unsafe, leave without explaining too much.

Workplace situations

Useful in a real Australian workplace.

Common situations

  • A coworker talks too much while you are working.
  • A coworker interrupts you repeatedly.
  • A coworker asks how much money you make.
  • A coworker asks intrusive questions about your marriage.
  • A coworker makes an inappropriate joke or comment.
  • A coworker keeps talking when you need to return to your station.

More examples

  • Someone keeps teasing you.
  • Someone stands too close and makes you uncomfortable.
  • Someone wants to keep talking after work when you want to go home.
  • A salesperson keeps pressuring you.
  • A stranger does not take a hint.
  • Someone asks you to do something you do not want to do.

Speaking practice

Say it out loud and make it sound natural.

  • 1. “Your coworker keeps talking while you are working. What do you say?”
  • 2. “Someone asks how much money you make. What do you say?”
  • 3. “Someone makes an inappropriate comment. What do you say?”
  • 4. “A salesperson keeps pressuring you. What do you say?”
  • 5. “You want to leave a conversation. What do you say?”
You do not need to memorize one exact sentence. Practice with your own words. Aim for calm, clear English that sounds natural to you.

Safety reminder

When safety comes first

Polite English is useful, but your safety is more important than being polite.

If someone is threatening, aggressive, following you, or making you feel unsafe:

  • Leave.
  • Move toward other people.
  • Go somewhere public or secure.
  • Ask someone you trust for help.
  • Contact emergency services when appropriate.

You do not owe someone a conversation just because they started one.

Final takeaway

Polite → Firm → Leave

Remember:

Start politely when the situation is safe. Set a clear boundary when necessary. Leave when you need to.

You can be kind without giving away your time, attention, or personal information.
